# 39. The player scaffold, the music face, and artwork colour

Date: 2026-07-31

## Status

Accepted.

## Context

`PlayerScreen` was the last surface still on the shell the rest of the app
left behind: a Material `Scaffold` with an app bar, a row of icon buttons
in it, a `Slider` for the seek bar, a `FilterChip` for silence trimming,
and `PopupMenuButton`s for the menus. The design system has had a
`PlayerScaffold` since the foundations phase - backdrop, hero, title
block, clusters, bottom region, and the landscape arrangement - and
nothing in the app used it.

Three things were waiting on this rebuild, each recorded:

- **The waveform seek bar had no client half.** The server side landed
  early (`GET /items/{pid}/waveform`), and the generated Dart model with
  it, but nothing read either.
- **The player's volume row was Material, not `WaxSlider`.** Recorded as
  converting with the screen, since a lone design-system slider under a
  Material seek bar would have converted nothing.
- **Artwork colour was declared and never extracted.** `WaxPalette`,
  `WaxAccent`, and `WaxBackdrop` all shipped with the foundations;
  the function that turns pixels into a palette did not, so every
  backdrop in the app drew its domain hue.

## Decision

### The palette is keyed by artwork URL, extracted inline, and bounded

The extractor is pure and takes RGBA bytes: it is a histogram weighted by
saturation and mid-tone value, and it answers **null** for artwork with no
usable colour rather than returning a grey. Null is a real answer - a
black-and-white sleeve, a scan of paper, a monochrome logo - and the
caller draws the domain hue for it, which is what `WaxPalette.forDomain`
is for.

Only the hue comes from the artwork. Saturation and lightness are clamped
by the system, because what reaches the eye is a tenth of the colour's
opacity over a fixed canvas, and an unclamped blown-out cover stops being
a backdrop. Glow and wash are two clamps of the same hue: dark shines
colour through darkness, light mixes it into paper, and one value cannot
do both.

Two deviations from the plan, both deliberate:

- **Keyed by artwork URL rather than by pid.** The colour is a property of
  the cover, so an album's twelve tracks extract once between them, and an
  item with no cover never creates an entry at all.
- **Extracted inline rather than in a `compute` isolate.** The decode
  already happens off the UI thread inside the engine; what is left is
  reading four thousand pixels, which is under a millisecond. Spawning an
  isolate costs more than the work it would carry, and Flutter web has no
  isolates to spawn - the plan's "isolate on native, inline on web" would
  have been two code paths for one arithmetic answer.

Results are held in an LRU of 128, remembered nulls included, and dropped
where the bytes behind a URL are dropped: sign-out, the artwork-cache
clear, a playlist cover regenerated by a membership write, a station logo
replaced by an edit. The accent is scoped to the player subtree through
`WaxAccent`, and it crossfades rather than cutting - it is the largest
thing on the screen, and a hard swap at a track boundary reads as a flash.

### The seek bar downsamples; the client normalises

The catalog stores a thousand buckets and a seek bar is whatever width it
was given. Drawing a thousand rectangles into three hundred pixels is a
thousand draw calls to paint a smear, so the bar reduces to one every
three logical pixels, each taking the **loudest** bucket it covers.
Loudest rather than mean: an averaged envelope loses exactly the
transients that make a track recognisable, and the shape a listener aims a
scrub at is its peaks.

The reduction is held by the seek bar's state, keyed on the peaks and the
width it was laid out at, rather than recomputed in `paint`. The playhead
moves several times a second and the envelope does not, so doing it per
frame would be a thousand comparisons and a fresh list to redraw a shape
that has not changed since the track started.

The values are normalised against the track's own loudest bucket, so a
quiet master draws a shape instead of a line along the axis. A file whose
every bucket is silence answers null rather than being divided by itself.

**Who asks is the caller's decision, not the provider's.** The music face
asks. A podcast episode is never analyzed upstream, and a book's bar spans
a chapter rather than the file, so a full-file envelope under it would be
the wrong shape - both would spend a round trip to be told "no" at best.
`pending` and `unavailable` both draw the plain bar and neither is polled:
`unavailable` is final, and `pending` changes only when an analyze pass
runs, which is not something a seek bar waits for.

### Only opaque pixels are read

`Image.toByteData(format: rawRgba)` hands back **premultiplied** bytes, so
a half-transparent crimson arrives as a half-brightness crimson. Feathered
edges, a rounded logo's antialiasing, and drop shadows are exactly that,
and counting them drags the reading toward black. So the histogram takes
fully opaque pixels only, where premultiplied and straight are the same
bytes. A pixel blended toward whatever is behind it is not a colour the
artwork has.

### The hero takes what is left, rather than a fraction of the window

Both readings had accumulated in the scaffold at once - a width clamp and
a height fraction, the second added after an overflow - and neither can be
right while what sits under the artwork varies by face. The music face
carries a volume row and an action row that the first sketch did not. So
the hero is an `Expanded` between the header and the clusters: it takes
the space nothing else wants, and disappears rather than overflowing on a
window with no room for it.

### The side-by-side arrangement is about height, not width class

5.3 names compact-landscape, and the reason it gives - a stack of clusters
under a hero does not fit - is a height problem that a 740x360 desktop
window has too without being compact. So the arrangement switches on
"wider than tall, and not much tall", and the hero there is sized by both
axes: the transport does not wrap and measures 272 px, so a column given
less than that overflows however small the artwork beside it is. The
artwork yields first and disappears rather than pushing controls off the
edge, and the column scrolls when a short window or a large text scale
leaves it less height than its own clusters need.

### Drag-to-dismiss moves the content, not the backdrop

A drag tracks the finger and a release becomes a spring that carries its
velocity, which is physics rather than a duration. Past a quarter of the
surface's height, or on a downward flick, it collapses and the route's own
exit takes over.

**It is a pointer gesture and nothing else.** Left in the semantics tree,
a vertical drag publishes `scrollUp` and `scrollDown` on the surface node,
and a screen reader's scroll then synthesises a drag long enough to cross
the dismiss threshold - so swiping to read the player would close it. The
gesture is excluded from semantics; the way out for a screen reader is the
collapse button, which says what it does.

What moves is the **content**, while the backdrop holds still. The player
is pushed over whatever was underneath and nothing paints there during the
gesture, so sliding the whole surface would drag a hole into view. The
backdrop is atmosphere; the content leaves through it.

Reduced motion does not turn the gesture off. What that setting turns off
is decoration, not the ability to put something back where it came from -
so the drag still tracks, and the release snaps instead of springing.

### One overflow, and the two things that stay outside it

The header is the collapse control, the provenance line, and - per 5.3 -
cast plus overflow, with the download control keeping its place beside
them because a player is where downloading what you are listening to
belongs. Add to playlist, share, and delete are rows of the one menu.
Delete became a function (`confirmDeleteItem`) as well as a button: a
second three-dot control beside the first would have been two menus
wearing one glyph.

The player is also a `Scaffold` with nothing in it but a body, for the one
thing a `Scaffold` does that no other widget does - it is what
`ScaffoldMessenger` presents into. The player is a route of its own over
the shell, so the chrome's own is not an ancestor, and every refusal
raised from here would assert without one.

### What the other three faces get, and what they do not

The music face is whole. Podcasts, books, and radio get the scaffold and
the controls they already had - speed, silence trimming, chapters - hosted
through its slots rather than left on a screen of their own. The speed
sheet, smart rewind, bookmarks, the chapter and transcript regions, and
the radio platter are the next phase's, and this one deliberately does not
approximate them.

Two departures from 5.3's per-face lists, both in the direction of not
removing something that works:

- **The sleep timer is on every face**, not only the spoken-word ones.
  Falling asleep to a record is what the control is for, and it is about
  the device rather than the medium.
- **Music has no speed chip**, which 5.3 also says, and the invariant that
  a music rate never persists is now pinned against the session rather
  than against a control that no longer exists.

## Consequences

- Every backdrop in the app that has artwork behind it now has a colour
  from it; the domain hue became the fallback it was always specified as
  rather than the only answer.
- The seek bar draws a real envelope for analyzed music. On a server whose
  analyze pass has never run - which is every server until someone runs it
  - it draws the plain bar and says nothing about why, which is the
  behaviour `pending` asks for.
- The star and rating row is still Material. Converting it wants a heart
  glyph the icon subset does not carry, and using the star glyph for both
  the star toggle and the rating stars would lose the distinction between
  them; it stays tracked in deferred work.
- The player's e2e handles moved with it: the transport gained the four it
  never had (previous, next, shuffle, repeat), the surface and the up-next
  peek gained their own, and add-to-playlist and share moved behind the
  overflow, which is one spec change.
